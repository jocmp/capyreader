    World of Warcraft: Forever Brings the Largest Visual Leap in the MMO's History

    With a corresponding rise to system requirements.

    • by William Judd
    • Thu, 3pm
    • Share:

    World of Warcraft stands as arguably the most successful MMO in gaming history, from its launch in 2004 to the accumulation of 11 (!) expansion packs over more than two decades of active development. That's left the underlying graphics engine a bit long in the tooth, which has finally prompted Blizzard to unveil World of Warcraft: Forever. This new game client pairs classic art and environments with advanced rendering techniques, including ray-traced global illumination (RTGI), to deliver the biggest visual leap in the franchise's history.

    It would be incorrect to say that WoW hasn't received graphics updates before now - over its long lifespan, Blizzard has periodically upgraded the engine with features like shadow maps, extended draw distances, screen-space sun shafts, per-pixel point lights and overhauled water rendering. Many of these features were subsequently ported over to World of Warcraft Classic upon its 2019 release. However, these piecemeal enhancements left core graphical limitations unresolved, including flat indirect lighting, static environmental shadows and primitive atmospheric scattering. World of Warcraft: Forever aims to systematically address these long-standing shortcomings to bring the classic world up to contemporary graphical standards.

    The defining technical advancement of World of Warcraft: Forever is its real-time diffuse RTGI. In exterior environments, this system generates striking differences in natural light bounce. For example, chapel walls visibly pick up green bounce light from surrounding grass and red hues from their roof shingles, while shadowed stone structures in Stormwind dynamically reflect the warm, golden tones of the setting sun rather than being bathed in a uniform, bluish ambient shadow.

    To maintain broad accessibility across a wide range of hardware, Blizzard implemented a custom probe-based ray tracer designed to run smoothly - even on older graphics cards lacking dedicated hardware ray-tracing support. While this coarse RTGI solution excels at calculating macro-level light bounce and ambient dispersal across expansive landscapes, it lacks per-pixel precision and fine-grained micro-occlusion. Consequently, minor visual artefacts can occasionally be observed in close-up scenarios, though the coarse approximation fits the vast scale of Azeroth effectively.

    In contrast to outdoor landscapes, artificially-lit interior spaces present a more modest graphical transformation. The new client relies on screen-space ambient occlusion (SSAO) alongside legacy-style vertex lighting to handle shadowed interiors, which can produce heavy shading around geometric intersections. Furthermore, Blizzard opted not to undertake a comprehensive physically-based rendering (PBR) overhaul of world materials, retaining original hand-painted diffuse textures to preserve the iconic visual style and charm of the 2004 original.

    Atmospheric quality receives a dramatic upgrade through a new volumetric lighting system that replaces old static distance fog. In eerie locations like Duskwood, thick volumetric fog scatters light from lanterns and trees, while green sun rays filter through pine canopies in Tirisfal Glades. Crucially, this volumetric fog interacts directly with dynamic weather patterns, creating shifting atmospheric effects that deepen immersion without compromising readability or playability.

      Environmental shadows and draw distances have also been thoroughly reworked. Previous game clients relied on fixed environmental shadows that remained static regardless of the sun's position. World of Warcraft: Forever introduces fully dynamic time-of-day shadowing, allowing shadows cast by mountains and structures to move realistically as the sun traverses the sky. Additionally, draw distances have been extended considerably, with volumetric scattering preserving the natural sense of world scale across distant terrain silhouettes.

      Water rendering sees another substantial upgrade via a new setting designated as "PBR Water". Oceans and lakes now feature dynamic wave systems, sharp specular highlights, screen-space reflections (SSR), underwater refraction, and realistic foam along shorelines. While these liquid effects are purely aesthetic and do not alter character physics, they transform open bodies of water - such as the Stormwind docks - into highly convincing natural environments.

      Performance testing on mid-tier hardware, such as an RTX 4070 laptop - roughly equivalent to a desktop RTX 4060 - running at 1080p on preset 7, we saw results around 70 to 80fps in open-world areas, though dense capital cities still experience frame-rate drops. Notably, the client currently lacks modern temporal upscaling solutions like DLSS or FSR, relying entirely on raw hardware power and traditional anti-aliasing methods.

      Ultimately, World of Warcraft: Forever represents a clever, respectful reworking that elevates classic artwork with modern lighting innovation while safeguarding the legacy of the original game.
